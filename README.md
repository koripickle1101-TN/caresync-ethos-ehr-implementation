## Project Purpose

CareSync Ethos is a Health IT implementation use case focused on patient safe digital change.

This project analyzes how a regional health care organization can replace an outdated charting system with a new electronic health record while protecting patients, supporting professionals, improving equitable access, and reducing workflow disruption.

The goal is not simply to launch new technology. The goal is to make sure the system is safe, usable, inclusive, and trusted before patients and professionals are expected to depend on it.

## Core Question

How can health care leaders move forward with digital transformation without rushing implementation, increasing provider burnout, or leaving patients behind?

## Strategic Problem

A regional health care organization is preparing to replace its outdated charting system with a new EHR. Leadership wants a faster and more connected platform for documentation, medication reconciliation, scheduling, patient messaging, and care coordination.

However, frontline professionals are concerned about workflow disruption, training time, alert fatigue, duplicate documentation, inbox overload, and patient safety during the transition.

From the patient perspective, a system change can feel invisible until something goes wrong. A medication list may not transfer correctly. A portal may be difficult to use. An appointment reminder may fail. A provider may spend more time searching the screen than listening.

These are not minor issues. They directly affect trust, safety, access, and quality of care.

## Key Insight

Technology should earn trust before it asks people to depend on it.

## Implementation Decision

In this use case, the organization decides to delay full launch by four weeks after pilot users identify medication reconciliation concerns and portal access issues for older adults.

Although this creates pressure on the project timeline, it prevents avoidable harm and gives the team time to improve training, correct workflows, validate patient safety processes, and add patient support resources.

## Recommendation

The organization should proceed with a phased EHR implementation using a patient safety first model.

The launch should not move forward until high risk workflows have been tested, frontline users have completed role specific training, patient access barriers have been addressed, downtime plans are ready, and post launch monitoring is active.

Success should be measured by patient safety, staff adoption, usability, access, workflow stability, and quality outcomes, not just whether the system went live on schedule.
